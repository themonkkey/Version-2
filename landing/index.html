The programme

Capacity Building Programme by Pahlé India Foundation

Who PIF is

Pahlé India Foundation is a homegrown, not-for-profit think-and-action tank founded by Dr. Rajiv Kumar, former Vice Chairman of NITI Aayog, on the principle of Putting India First to Make India First. PIF does not just recommend what should be done; it partners to ensure it gets done — rigorous, evidence-based analysis rooted in Indian realities, carried through to implementation.

What the programme is

A first-of-its-kind, government-led capacity building initiative that PIF designed and delivered for the Planning Department, Government of Andhra Pradesh. The state targets a Rs 308 lakh crore economy by 2047 — the most ambitious growth target in India — and reaching it needs every official, Secretary to mandal officer, fluent in how output is measured and driven.

It runs on a 3-month cascading model: two-day district trainings, then constituency-level and master trainer rounds, across all 5 zones and 28 districts — about 980 district officials, over 6,125 constituency officials and around 2,750 master trainers. Sessions cover the 2047 vision, GSDP / GDDP / GMDP methodology, boosting GVA, the 645 KPIs, district profiling and case studies.

What we achieved

Between 16 June and 6 August 2026, 141 sessions were delivered across 18 districts with 6,482 recorded attendances, at all three levels — district, constituency and mandal, and master trainers. Every session was filed as a status report, and that record, its photographs and the baseline and endline assessments are all on this platform.

Methodology

How a district's income is estimated

The official method, from state total to rupees per person, as taught in the Swarna Andhra capacity building programme.

Source: Directorate of Economics & Statistics, Andhra Pradesh. Base year 2022-23.

01

Measurement exists to be used, not filed

National income is the total value of everything a territory produces in a year. The accounts built on it are working instruments, not statistical housekeeping.

Planning

Investment and tax policy, and budget allocation aimed at the things holding growth back.

Monitoring

Tracking whether plans are actually raising productivity, output and welfare.

Comparison

Setting one region against another on the same basis, which is what makes a gap arguable.

02

Value added is what is left after inputs

Gross Value Added is the single building block everything else is assembled from.

Output − Intermediate Consumption = GVA
Illustrative exampleAmount
Value of rice producedRs 100
Seeds, fertiliser, electricity, transport and other inputsRs 40
Gross Value AddedRs 60
Higher output does not always mean higher value added. If input costs rise faster than output, or prices fall, GVA can shrink while production grows.

GVA is measured at basic prices, before product taxes and subsidies enter the figure.

03

Three routes to the same total

An economy can be counted by what it makes, by what it pays out, or by what it spends. Done correctly the three agree, which is what makes them useful as cross-checks.

Production

Output less intermediate consumption, sector by sector. The most intuitive route for linking sector data to GVA, and the one used for most of Andhra Pradesh.

Income

Compensation of employees, operating surplus, mixed income, plus taxes less subsidies on production. Used for government services and organised sectors.

Expenditure

Consumption, investment, government spending and net exports. Rarely available below the national level.

04

One economy, measured at four levels

The same activity is counted nationally, by state, by district and by mandal. Data availability falls and estimation difficulty rises at every step down.

05

Sixteen sub-sectors, and the state groups them differently

The Government of India and Andhra Pradesh cover the same sixteen sub-sectors but draw the boundary in different places. Andhra Pradesh moves Mining and Quarrying out of Primary and into Industry.

GroupGovernment of IndiaAndhra Pradesh
First group Primary: crops, livestock, forestry and logging, fishing and aquaculture, mining and quarrying Agriculture: crops, livestock, forestry and logging, fishing and aquaculture
Second group Secondary: manufacturing, electricity and utilities, construction Industry: mining and quarrying, manufacturing, electricity and utilities, construction
Third group Tertiary: the eight service sub-sectors Services: the same eight service sub-sectors
Andhra Pradesh splits Crops into Agriculture and Horticulture when reporting, which is why district tables on this site show seventeen lines rather than sixteen.
06

Where district data exists, measure it; where it does not, apportion

Neither approach wins outright. The official method uses each where its data holds.

Top-down

Estimate the state total first, then distribute it across districts on a suitable indicator. Keeps districts consistent with the state figure, but will not capture local variation.

Bottom-up

Measure each district directly from local production, enterprise and administrative records, then add them up. Reflects local conditions, but demands reliable district data.

Mixed, in practice

Bottom-up wherever district data is reliable, top-down apportionment everywhere else. This is what Andhra Pradesh actually does.

07

From value added to rupees per person

Four moves take a sector's value added to the number an officer is asked about in a review.

08

The same year is published five times, each firmer than the last

A year's first figure arrives before its records are complete. Each later release replaces projection with measurement, so two figures for the same year can differ legitimately.

09

District estimates are harder, and the method says so

More local detail usually means more data constraints. Four of them shape every district figure.

Data availability

Some sector data exists only at state level, or carries weak district identifiers.

Informality

A large share of activity is unregistered and never enters routine reporting systems.

Headquarter effects

Company and service records are booked where the office is, not where the work happens.

Proxy dependence

Where direct data is missing, allocation keys and proxy indicators stand in for measurement.

The core rule: use the same sectoral logic as the state accounts wherever district data exists, and apportion the state estimate on the best available indicator where it does not.
10

Mandal figures are district figures, shared out by indicator

A mandal is not measured directly. Its district's value added is distributed on an indicator that tracks where the activity actually sits.

Five things to carry back

1
GVA measures value added, not production, sales or expenditure.
2
Always read an estimate with its source, method and revision stage. A figure without its vintage cannot be compared.
3
Validation is central. Triangulate the official estimate against administrative data and proxies.
4
Measurement becomes management only through assigned follow-up.
5
Data should not end at reporting. It should improve diagnosis, prioritisation and administrative decisions.
