About

What this assistant is

The Swarna Andhra GVA Assistant is a question-and-answer tool built by Pahlé India Foundation for the state capacity-building programme. It helps district, constituency, and mandal officers find clear answers about how state and district income is measured, and how to raise it.

Who it is for

  • District and constituency officers who need quick, reliable answers grounded in official material.
  • Master trainers preparing sessions on estimation methodology.
  • Planning teams looking at where a district has comparative advantage and what to do about it.

How it works

Every question runs a semantic search across the full corpus, the official DDP and GSVA source documents, the district data, and all 1,378 vision plans. Answers are generated from that material and always cite the source, so every claim can be traced back.

Where the answers come from

  • The official Guideline for Compilation of DDP Estimates from the National Statistics Office.
  • The Compilation of GSVA Estimates with base year 2022-23.
  • District GSDP, GDDP, and per-capita data for all 28 districts.
  • Vision and action plans for every constituency and mandal, plus sector case studies.
Methodology

How a district's
income is estimated

The official method, from state total to rupees per person. Five chapters, following the National Statistics Office guideline for District Domestic Product.

Scroll
01
What district income measures
01

One state total

Gross State Domestic Product is a single number for all of Andhra Pradesh: the value of everything produced in the state in a year.

Useful for comparing states. Useless for deciding where to build a cold-storage unit.

02

Twenty-eight parts

District Domestic Product breaks that total apart. Each district gets its own estimate, built from its own economy.

DDP is to a district what GSDP is to a state and GDP is to the country. Same idea, smaller frame.
03

One district, in focus

Once a district has its own number, it can be compared, tracked across years, and divided by population to give per-capita income.

All 28 districts, four years each, are in the corpus behind this assistant.
02
The eleven industries
04

The economy, divided

Estimation never treats a district as one lump. It is split into eleven broad industry groups, each measured on its own terms.

05

Primary

Agriculture and allied activities: crops, livestock, forestry, fishing. Measured from production volumes and prices.

In most AP districts this is where comparative advantage shows up first.
06

Secondary

Mining, manufacturing, electricity and utilities, construction. Estimated from registered output, sales tax records and workforce data.

07

Tertiary

Trade and hotels, transport and communication, financial services, real estate, public administration, other services. The hardest to measure and the fastest growing.

Six of the eleven groups are services, which is why service-sector indicators drive so much of the estimate.
03
How the numbers are built
08

Top-down

Start with the state total, then share it out across districts using an indicator that tracks where the activity actually happened: workforce, output, or registered units.

Used wherever direct district data does not exist.

09

Bottom-up

Go the other way. Measure each district directly from local data, then add them up. The state total is whatever the districts sum to.

Bottom-up reflects local conditions best, so it is preferred wherever the data supports it.
10

Mixed, in practice

Neither approach wins outright. The official method uses bottom-up for industries with reliable district data and top-down for the rest.

Accuracy where possible, coverage everywhere else.
04
From value added to income
11

Gross Value Added

Each industry's contribution, measured at basic prices: the value it produces, less the cost of what it consumed producing it.

GVA is measured at basic prices, before product taxes and subsidies enter.
12

Summed across sectors

Add the adjusted GVA of all eleven industries and you have Gross District Value Added.

GDVA = sum of adjusted GVA across all sectors
13

To district product

Add taxes on products, subtract subsidies on products. That shift from basic prices to market prices is what separates value added from domestic product.

GDDP = GDVA + Taxes on Products − Subsidies on Products
14

To income per person

Subtract consumption of fixed capital for the net figure, then divide by mid-year projected population.

Per capita district income, in rupees per person: the number most often quoted, and most often quoted without its base year.
05
How final is the figure
15

Published early

A year's first figure arrives before the year's records are complete. The First Advance Estimate is built from part-year indicators, so planning does not have to wait for the accounts to close.

2025-26 carries FAE wherever it appears on this site.
16

Revised as records arrive

As crop surveys, tax records and filings come in, projection is replaced with measurement and the figure is re-issued. First Revised, then Second Revised.

2024-25 stands at FRE, and 2023-24 at SRE.
17

Settled

By the third revision nearly all source records are in. The Third Revised Estimate is the closest the series comes to a final figure.

2022-23 carries TRE, the firmest year in the four-year window.
18

Read the label

The same year can be quoted at different stages, and the stages differ. That is why every figure in this assistant names its year and its estimate class together.

A growth rate that mixes an FAE with a TRE compares a forecast with a measurement.
