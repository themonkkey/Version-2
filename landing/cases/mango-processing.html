Overview

Why Mango Processing Matters for Andhra Pradesh Districts

Andhra Pradesh is the largest mango producer in India, with over 31 lakh MT produced annually across Chittoor, Vizianagaram, Kadapa, Anantapur and Srikakulam. Yet only a fraction is processed — the rest is sold as fresh fruit at volatile prices or lost to gluts, pests and post-harvest spoilage. Processing converts that risk into income.

Shelf-life

Processing converts perishable mango into pulp, slice, pickle and amchur with a 12–24 month shelf life.

Waste-to-wealth

35–60% of the mango is peel and kernel, which can become pectin, mango butter and animal feed.

Women and SHGs

Processing creates cottage-level slicing, pickling, packaging and grading jobs for women SHGs.

Local value

Pulp, pickle and brand value stay inside the mandal or cluster, not just the metropolis.

CASE STUDY 1 — PHILIPPINES DRIED MANGO EXPORT CLUSTER

A Brand-Led Export Model

Profood International Corporation, founded by Justin Uy in Cebu, has grown into the largest Philippine-based dried fruit producer, employing more than 8,000 workers across its factories. The cluster spans Cebu for branding and packaging and Mindanao for farm sourcing, turning a seasonal tropical fruit into a year-round branded export category sold in more than 60 countries.

03

HOW THE PHILIPPINES MODEL WORKS

From Farm to Export Shelf

The Profood model is a four-stage chain that converts a perishable fruit into a 24-month-shelf-life branded export SKU.

Contract farming clusters

Profood ties up with mango growers in Mindanao for off-take at pre-agreed prices, ensuring steady raw material supply and farmer income stability through the six-month harvest window.

IPM and GlobalGAP certification

Farms follow Integrated Pest Management with fruit-bagging, residue testing and traceability. Certification unlocks export to the US, EU, Japan and Korea, where residue limits are tight.

Centralised processing plant

The Cebu plant handles washing, peeling, slicing, osmotic dehydration and freeze-drying, metal detection and nitrogen-flush packaging — one site, one quality standard, year-round output.

Brand and multi-channel export

Branded products ship to more than 60 countries via supermarket chains, duty-free shops and e-commerce. Brand margin is three to five times higher than bulk pulp.

THE INSTITUTIONAL MODEL

FPO-Led Aggregation and Processing

The chain runs from grower to market: small and marginal farmers in cluster villages feed a Farmer Producer Company that aggregates raw fruit and runs a common brand; a pickle, pulp or slice processing unit owned or leased by the FPC and FSSAI-licensed does the conversion; NHB, KVK and CFTRI provide recipes, IPM, packaging and certification support; and produce moves through APMC wholesale, retail pouches and Gulf export channels.

Funding and scheme support

The NHB Cluster Development Programme, PMFME capital subsidy, NABARD credit and MoFPI cold chain support stack together.

The standard FPO processing unit package

An indicative package for a one MT per day pickle and pulp FPO unit: a 1,500–2,000 sq ft shed, stainless-steel washing and sorting tables, two pulpers, a paste mill and mixer, three jacketed tanks, a retort or pasteuriser for RTS beverage, a pouch-sealing and labelling machine, two cold storage chests, crates, drums and a basic lab kit, plus the FSSAI licence, packaging artwork and brand mark.

Indicative capex

Rs 18–25 lakh per unit before the 35% PMFME subsidy; working capital Rs 8–10 lakh per season.

07

MANGO WASTE-TO-WEALTH

Every Component Gets a Use

During industrial mango processing, 35–60% of the fruit becomes by-product — peel, kernel, scutcher and sap. Peer-reviewed research confirms these are rich in pectin, polyphenols, carotenoids and arachidic acid, all monetisable.

Peel

Pectin extraction, polyphenol and carotenoid extract, dietary fibre, natural colourant and compost.

Kernel

Mango butter for cosmetics, edible oil rich in arachidic acid, animal feed and starch.

Scutcher and fibre

Animal feed, compost, vermi-compost and bio-pellets or fuel.

Sap and latex

Traditional dye base, adhesive and medicinal or herbal use.

The working principle

Localise bulky primary processing — pulp, peel-drying, kernel crushing — at cluster level; centralise advanced extraction of pectin, mango butter and polyphenols at district or state level where capital and lab capacity exist.

08

THE AP CLUSTER TODAY

From Bulk Pulp to Branded Diversification

Andhra Pradesh already has one of India's largest mango processing clusters — about 45 pulp units, 36 in Chittoor and 9 in Tirupati, processing mainly Totapuri into pulp for export to West Asia. Recent geopolitical disruption left around 3 lakh tonnes of pulp stranded, pointing to the need for product diversification, branding and domestic retail channels.

The impact pathway

The farmer earns a stable off-take price via FPO contract rather than only the APMC spot price. SHG-run pickle, slice and pulp units generate 30–50 jobs per cluster. Peel and kernel return to farming as compost or are sold as pectin and butter feedstock, and brand plus retail-pack captures three to five times the margin of bulk pulp export.

The message for AP

Review each mango cluster not only for area and production but also for processing potential and by-product monetisation. The stranded-pulp crisis of 2024 is the argument for diversification.

LESSONS FOR AP DISTRICT AND MANDAL OFFICIALS

Six Lessons That Translate Directly

The Philippines and Malihabad cases converge on six lessons that sequence what to do first, what to standardise and what to measure — so that machinery purchase does not precede market and raw-material readiness.

Start with clusters

Select mango-dense mandals in Chittoor, Tirupati, Vizianagaram and Kadapa so raw-material aggregation is viable. A cluster of 1,000 ha within a 25 km radius is the minimum viable unit.

Keep first processing local

Bulky mango fruit cannot travel long distances economically. Pulp, slice, pickle and amchur should be processed within 30 km of harvest to cut losses and retain local jobs.

Use SHGs and FPOs

Women SHGs and farmer FPOs are the ideal operators for pickle, slice, amchur and cottage pulp units. The FPO structure gives aggregation, working capital and brand identity.

Standardise products

Recipes, packaging, labels, the FSSAI licence, batch tracking and quality control are non-negotiable. Without them there is no retail shelf, no export order and no repeat buyer.

Converge schemes

Horticulture, DRDA and SHG support, the PMFME 35% capital subsidy, APEDA export support, MSME credit, the NHB cluster programme and KVK technical support — stack, do not silo.

Track outcomes

Measure income to farmers, jobs created, MT processed and sold, repeat buyers and export value, in a monthly review by the Joint Director of Horticulture and the DRDA Project Director.

10

HOW AP DISTRICTS CAN ADAPT THE MODELS

Three Parallel Tracks

Fresh mango processing, by-product monetisation and district-level enablers should run together. The aim is to convert Chittoor and Vizianagaram from bulk-pulp clusters into branded, diversified mango hubs.

Fresh mango processing

Convert the harvest into branded SKUs: pulp in aseptic drums and retail pouches, slice and IQF for HoReCa and QSR chains, SHG-led pickle, amchur for the spice trade, candy and bars for school and tourism retail, and RTS beverage and nectar.

By-product monetisation

Turn 35–60% of the fruit into revenue: peel to pectin and polyphenol extract at district level, kernel to mango butter at cluster and central level, scutcher to animal feed and vermi-compost at village level, and compost returned to the mango orchard to close the loop.

District-level enablers

A district mango value-chain map and cluster atlas, technical tie-ups with KVK, NRCB, CFTRI and IIHR, a common AP brand with FSSAI hand-holding and a packaging kit, buyer linkages with retail chains and APEDA buyers, the PMFME subsidy and bank credit stack, and a monthly Horticulture and DRDA KPI review.

The ideal first step

Identify one mango cluster per district and run a 100-day pilot — one pulp and pickle FPO unit, one brand, one buyer tie-up — before scaling.

THE MONITORING CHECKLIST

What to Review Every Month

A monthly KPI review by the Joint Director of Horticulture and the DRDA Project Director keeps the mango cluster on track, in three tiers.

Input and process KPIs

Mango farmers, SHGs and FPOs mobilised; fruit aggregated in MT; trainees completing practical training; FSSAI and APEDA registrations completed; unit utilisation and days of operation; and hectares under IPM and GlobalGAP practice.

Output KPIs

Pulp, pickle, slice and amchur produced and sold with value realised; SKUs and brands launched; peel and kernel sent for by-product use; compost returned to farms; and export shipments dispatched.

Outcome KPIs

Additional income to farmers and SHGs; jobs created for women and rural youth; reduction in post-harvest loss; repeat buyers and market expansion; brand premium captured over the bulk-pulp price; and cluster-level mango price stabilisation against the APMC spot.

The closing message

For district and mandal officials, the mango case studies show how a crop-specific value chain can move from production monitoring to income, enterprise and employment generation. The Philippines case proves branding scales a single fruit into a global category; Malihabad proves an FPO cluster can run a profitable pickle, pulp and amchur mix. AP's Chittoor and Vizianagaram clusters have the raw material — they need the brand, the FPO discipline and the by-product line.
