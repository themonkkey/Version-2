Biofloc Tilapia Farming

A replicable model for mandal-level income generation, based on CMFRI's Scheduled Caste Sub-Plan initiative.

CMFRI, KeralaAquaculture livelihoodsReplicable model

THE CONCEPT

What is Biofloc Fish Farming?

Biofloc is a high-density fish farming method carried out in a controlled tank, where naturally occurring microbes convert fish waste into useful protein-rich nutrients — recycled back as feed.

Controlled environment

Farming happens in a single tank, not an open pond — usable even where no water body exists.

Waste becomes feed

Microbial 'floc' converts fish waste into nutrients, cutting feed cost and pollution.

High density, small space

Large yields from a compact footprint set up beside the household.

WHY IT SUITS RURAL HOUSEHOLDS · No pond or river required · Set up next to the home · Low land requirement

Family-run, low labour · Steady 8-month income cycle · Reuses water & feed efficiently · THE CASE STUDY

Cheranellur, Ernakulam — The Numbers · Scheduled Caste families · formed one self-help group

1,800 · GIFT tilapia fry · stocked in a single tank · 23,500 L · Biofloc tank · built beside their homes

₹1,35,000 · Income per cycle

≈ $1,800 every 8 months

Fish reach 300–500 g in a single 8-month cycle.

A previously landless group now runs a viable micro-enterprise — proof that the model creates real, repeatable household income without access to open water.

THE MECHANICS · How the CMFRI Model Was Delivered

Identify & organise

Select eligible SC families and form a self-help group (SHG).

Build the unit

Install a 23,500 L biofloc tank next to households.

Stock the fry

Source GIFT tilapia fry from a certified hatchery (MPEDA).

Manage & monitor

Use a water-quality kit; CMFRI guides every growth phase.

Harvest & sell

Harvest at 300–500 g; earn ₹1.35 lakh per 8-month cycle.

CMFRI provided the tank, fry, feed and continuous technical guidance — the families provided labour and ownership.

WHY IT MATTERS · A Tool for Inclusive Rural Development

Reaches the landless

Designed under the Scheduled Caste Sub-Plan to benefit families without access to open water bodies.

Builds entrepreneurs

Families become small-scale business owners, not just beneficiaries — income stays in the community.

Environmentally sound

Closed, water-efficient system that recycles waste and avoids polluting local water sources.

Already proven & scaling

Running across Palakkad, Thrissur, Idukki, Kottayam & Kollam under CMFRI guidance.

ACTION PLAN · Replication Roadmap for Your Mandal · PHASE 1 · Survey & Select · Map SC/landless households lacking water-body access

Identify motivated families willing to form an SHG · Confirm space (~40 m²) and power/water near homes

PHASE 2 · Mobilise & Fund · Form & register the self-help group · Tap SCSP / state fishery & NRLM convergence funds

Link group to a bank account for the cycle · PHASE 3 · Set Up & Train · Install biofloc tank; supply fry, feed & test kit

Hands-on training on water quality & feeding · Source GIFT fry from a certified hatchery · PHASE 4

Run & Scale · Mentor through the first 8-month cycle · Track growth, costs & sales as a demo unit

Use success to onboard more groups · FOR THE OFFICER · Key Factors for a Successful Replication

Convergence

Combine SCSP, fishery dept & NRLM schemes for capital and subsidy.

Technical hand-holding

Tie up with a research/KVK partner for continuous guidance like CMFRI.

Quality inputs

Use certified GIFT fry and reliable feed — the single biggest yield driver.

Water discipline

Daily water-quality monitoring decides survival and growth rates.

Group ownership

A genuine SHG with shared stake sustains the unit beyond officials.

Demo-led scaling

Run one model unit well, then let visible income recruit the next groups.

Pick one ward. Form one group. Build one tank. Prove the income — then replicate.
