Welcome to the series

Measuring the economy, district by district

A new five-part bulletin for the officers who compile and use Andhra Pradesh’s economic numbers. This opening edition introduces the programme, then puts the GVA practice sheets in your hands, with one worked example from field data to a finished figure.

“Officials should rely on data-based decision-making and use economic indicators such as GSDP and CAGR when framing policy, the true measure of development is not higher growth figures alone, but how much that growth improves the lives of the poor.”

- Shri N. Chandrababu Naidu, Hon’ble Chief Minister of Andhra Pradesh, at the ‘Economic Intelligence for Governance’ workshop, Tirupati, July 2026
What this is

A programme to strengthen how the state measures its economy

Pahlé India Foundation’s Centre for Data for Economic Decision-making (CoDED) is working with the Directorate of Economics & Statistics (DES), Planning Department, Government of Andhra Pradesh, reviewing the methods behind GSDP, district (GDDP) and mandal estimates, rationalising the state’s economic indicators, and building the capacity of the officers who produce and use these numbers. The work is anchored to the Swarna Andhra @2047 vision.

This bulletin is where that work reaches your desk. Across the series it will walk through the practical tools you can use, worked case studies, what officers told us, and the vision and action plans that put the numbers to work, before a closing edition brings it all together in a live dashboard and assistant.

The practice sheets

Three practice sheets, one shared method

Why these sheets? They turn GVA estimation from a black box into something you can see and check. Each one shows how district data on production, prices and inputs becomes a sectoral GVA estimate, keeps the methodology transparent (the components, assumptions, rates, ratios and adjustments behind the number), and lets you see how each input moves the final figure. That makes the estimate easier to monitor and to improve.

Now to the first tool you can keep on your desk. District Agriculture GVA is built from three sub-sectors, Crop, Livestock and Fishery, and there is a separate practice sheet (calculator) for each. They look a little different, but every one follows the same four steps. Each sheet also opens with a short Before you start tab that states its purpose and how to use it:

  1. Value of Output: for each item, Production × Price. The sheet totals these into the Gross Value of Output.
  2. Less inputs: subtract intermediate consumption, the costs used up in production, to get unadjusted GVA.
  3. Less FISIM: remove the imputed financial-services charge (each sheet handles this slightly differently).
  4. Adjusted GVA: the final, reportable figure the sheet produces automatically.
