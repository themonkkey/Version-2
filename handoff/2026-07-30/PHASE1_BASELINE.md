# Phase 1 baseline — hard retrieval benchmark

_Recorded 2026-07-28. Index: `cohere:embed-english-v3.0`, 59,388 chunks (unchanged)._

Every later phase is judged against these numbers. Generated by `gen_hard_retrieval.py`,
measured by `run_hard_retrieval.py`, per-prompt log in `hard_retrieval_results.jsonl`.

## The set

180 prompts, six strata, ground truth is a specific `(source, page)` chunk. Mean lexical
overlap between question and gold chunk is **0.28**, so a keyword matcher cannot pass;
retrieval has to be semantic.

## Headline

**recall@10 = 38.9%** (70/180), CI [32.1, 46.2] on the current production configuration.

The existing gold benchmark reports 100% retrieval. That number is an artifact: every
numeric gold prompt names a district, `detect_district` fires, and `retrieve` force-injects
that district's snapshot at score 1.0, so the target figure is in the context by
construction. It measures the forcing rule, not the retriever. This set has real headroom.

## Results by configuration

recall %, `full` = current production, `dense_only` = both hand-tuned rules disabled.

| config | R@1 | R@3 | R@5 | R@10 | docR@10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| full | 6 | 16 | 22 | 39 | 69 |
| no_force | 9 | 17 | 22 | 40 | 70 |
| no_rescue | 10 | 23 | 32 | 39 | 74 |
| dense_only | **16** | **26** | **32** | 40 | **74** |

## Finding 1 — the two hand-tuned rules are net negative

Exact McNemar on paired outcomes, `full` vs `dense_only`:

| | full wins | dense_only wins | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R@1 | 8 | 26 | **0.0029** |
| R@3 | 10 | 28 | **0.0051** |
| R@5 | 10 | 28 | **0.0051** |
| R@10 | 10 | 12 | 0.83 |

The district force-injection (`app.py:196`) and the keyword rescue (`app.py:238`) inject
chunks at scores 1.0 and 0.99, which places them above everything the embeddings ranked.
When those injected chunks are not the ones that answer the question, they occupy the top
slots and push the correct chunk down. At recall@10 the effect washes out (p=0.83); in the
top few slots — which is what actually reaches the LLM under the current context cap — they
cost roughly **10 points**.

Worst cases: `method_paraphrase` R@1 goes 0% → 10% and `district_profile` R@1 goes 10% → 35%
once the rules are off.

This contradicts the assumption behind both rules. They were added to fix specific observed
failures and were never measured against a set that could show their cost.

**Consequence for Phase 4:** the plan was to replace the keyword rescue with BM25. The
evidence says remove it first and treat BM25 as a new proposal that must earn its place
against this baseline, rather than as a drop-in replacement for something assumed useful.

## Finding 2 — 34 points are trapped in chunking, not embeddings

At `dense_only`: the right **document** is retrieved for **134/180 (74%)** of prompts, but
the right **chunk** for only **72/180 (40%)**.

**62 prompts — 34 points — retrieve the correct document and the wrong page.** That is a
chunking and page-granularity failure, not an embedding failure. It is the single largest
identified loss, and it is exactly what Phase 3 targets:

- 6,888 chunks exceed their folder's size cap; for `methodology` it is 219 of 253 (87%),
  because `sub_split` only breaks on `\n\n` and never splits an over-long paragraph.
- Tables are flattened to `" | ".join(cells)`, so data rows lose their headers.

The correlation holds per stratum: `methodology`, the most oversized folder, has the widest
doc-vs-chunk gap (70% vs 32%).

## Finding 3 — `training_case` fails at the document level

`training_case` docR@10 is **13-27%** — the wrong document entirely, not the wrong page. It
is the only stratum failing this way, and it is the only folder whose chunks get the 4x
repeated keyword block prepended at `ingest.py:166`. That is now a suspect with evidence
behind it, not just a style objection. n=15, so treat as a strong hint rather than settled.

`ambiguous_mandal` is the sharpest instance of Finding 2: 95% right document, 25% right
chunk. Disambiguating which mandal is being asked about works; locating the answer inside
that document does not.

## Honest limitation

Chunk-level recall **understates** true answerability. A question generated from page 100
may also be answerable from page 99, which this metric scores as a miss. True end-to-end
performance sits somewhere between the chunk figure (40%) and the document figure (74%).

Both are far below the 100% the old benchmark reported, so the headroom is real either way.
The metric's value is as a **consistent yardstick across phases**, not as an absolute
statement of quality. Compare phases to each other, not to the gold set's 100%.
