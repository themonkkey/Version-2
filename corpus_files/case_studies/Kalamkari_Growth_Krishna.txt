Successful Models Kalamkari Can Emulate for Growth

A practical case study deck for mandal officers in Krishna district

Focus: Pedana–Machilipatnam Kalamkari — growth, tourism, digital markets, aggregation, GI branding and clean production

1

Core Idea: Build a Hybrid Kalamkari Growth Model

No single case is enough; Krishna should combine the best lessons from successful Indian craft and textile clusters.

Bagru Block Printing

Closest craft-process model: block printing + washing + common environmental infrastructure.

Raghurajpur Heritage Village

Craft tourism model: turn production into a visitor experience and village identity.

Chanderiyaan, Chanderi

Digital market model: design archive, e-commerce and youth-led digital skills.

Tiruppur ZLD

Compliance model: common treatment, reuse and monitoring for water-sensitive steps.

Recommended model for Krishna: Bagru-style cluster infrastructure + Raghurajpur-style craft tourism + Chanderiyaan-style digital access + Tiruppur-lite water management.

2

THE KRISHNA DISTRICT BASELINE

Strong Identity, Unorganised Growth

The challenge is to move from dispersed craft production to a managed, market-ready and sustainable cluster.

Current strengths

ODOP identity and GI recognition. Vegetable dye and wooden block heritage. A product range of sarees, rugs, towels, paintings, bed covers and clothing material, geographically concentrated around Pedana and nearby centres.

Growth bottlenecks

Fragmented producers and weak order aggregation. Limited contemporary product development. Water-use and wastewater risks in the washing and dyeing steps. Inconsistent packaging, quality assurance and digital marketing, and low linkage with tourism, exports and premium buyers.

3

CASE STUDY 1 — BAGRU BLOCK PRINTING, RAJASTHAN

The Common-Infrastructure Model

Best fit for Kalamkari because Bagru is also a traditional hand block-printing cluster.

What the model did

Built common infrastructure for a craft-based textile cluster, used a common effluent treatment approach with zero-discharge intent, recycled and reused a large share of wastewater, and protected the credibility of hand block printing as an eco-sensitive craft.

Lesson for Krishna officials

Create a common Kalamkari washing and dyeing facility rather than unit-level ETPs. Anchor it around the Pedana–Machilipatnam craft identity. Link environmental compliance with branding as water-responsible Kalamkari, and run cluster-level governance through an artisan committee or SPV.

4

CASE STUDY 2 — RAGHURAJPUR HERITAGE CRAFTS VILLAGE, ODISHA

The Craft Tourism Model

Best model for converting a craft cluster into a visitor experience and a local tourism economy.

What the model did

Positioned the village itself as the product. Visitors experience artisans, homes, studios and live demonstrations; craft became a tourism asset rather than only a retail product, and local identity was built around a recognised art tradition.

Action for Krishna

Develop a Pedana Kalamkari Craft Trail. Create visitor-friendly demo centres and retail outlets, train artisans to host paid workshops for schools, tourists and designers, and link the Kalamkari trail with Machilipatnam beach and cultural circuits.

5

CASE STUDY 3 — CHANDERIYAAN, CHANDERI, MADHYA PRADESH

The Digital Market Model

Best model for digital empowerment, design cataloguing and e-commerce in a traditional textile cluster.

What the model did

Built an ICT resource centre for weavers, supported digital literacy, product cataloguing and market access, used a design archive and e-commerce to reduce dependence on local intermediaries, and engaged youth in cluster modernisation.

Action for Krishna

Create a Kalamkari digital design bank of motifs, blocks, borders and colourways. Train youth in product photography, cataloguing, QR stories and online listings. Onboard producer groups on ONDC, GeM, Amazon Karigar and state emporia platforms, and make every product traceable to artisan, process and GI authenticity.

6

CASE STUDY 4 — TIRUPPUR ZLD AS A COMPLIANCE BENCHMARK

Common Treatment, Reuse and Monitoring

Do not copy Tiruppur's scale directly; copy its principle of common treatment, reuse and monitoring.

Technical lesson

Wastewater is collected at cluster level. Common treatment and recycling reduce freshwater dependence, monitoring and compliance are built into the operating model, and salt and sludge management must be planned from the beginning.

Action for Kalamkari

Start with common washing, dyeing and wastewater collection points. Install a mini-CETP or closed-loop treatment facility after an effluent audit, reuse treated water for first-stage washing and cleaning, and use APPCB-approved monitoring before scaling to advanced ZLD modules.

THE PROPOSED MODEL

Pedana Kalamkari Integrated Craft Growth Model

A practical package combining infrastructure, tourism, digital marketing, aggregation, GI and clean production.

Common facility centre

Dye preparation, washing, drying, testing, packaging and mini-effluent treatment.

Craft tourism trail

Live demo centre, workshops, craft cafe, retail outlet and school and tourist visits.

Digital marketplace

Design bank, QR traceability, product catalogues and online platform onboarding.

Producer enterprise

Aggregation, order management, quality standardisation and payment systems.

GI and authenticity

Certified Machilipatnam Kalamkari tags, artisan registration and an anti-imitation campaign.

Water-responsible production

Water audit, common treatment, reuse, a sludge and salt protocol and APPCB monitoring.

8

WHAT MANDAL OFFICERS CAN DO

Six Steps in the Kalamkari Centres

Translate the case studies into local action in Pedana, Machilipatnam, Polavaram, Kappaladoddi and nearby production points.

Map and mobilise

List artisans, SHGs, workshops, water-use steps, current markets and infrastructure gaps.

Create a common platform

Facilitate an artisan association, SPV or producer company for orders, quality and shared assets.

Prepare two DPRs

One for a craft growth common facility centre and one for common washing and mini-CETP water management.

Build market linkages

Connect with designers, boutiques, hotel chains, AP Tourism, Lepakshi, ONDC, GeM and export councils.

Launch a tourism pilot

Identify 10–15 demonstration-ready artisan homes and workshops and one common visitor centre.

Track KPIs monthly

Artisan income, orders, tourist visits, digital sales, treated water, reuse rate and GI-tagged products.

9

RECOMMENDATIONS AND TRACKING INDICATORS

The Action Checklist After Training

Use this as the action checklist after the training session.

Top recommendations

Adopt Bagru as the anchor model with common facilities for the block-printing cluster. Create a Pedana Kalamkari craft trail before peak tourism seasons. Build a Kalamkari digital design bank and online catalogue. Form a producer enterprise to handle quality, orders and payments. Start water management with an audit, common washing and a mini-CETP pilot, and use GI tags and QR stories to fight imitation and support premium pricing.

KPIs to review monthly

Number of artisans mapped and onboarded. Number of GI-tagged and QR-traceable products. Digital sales value and buyer inquiries. Tourist and workshop footfall and direct sales. Share of products passing quality and colour-fastness checks. Wastewater treated and the share of treated water reused. Average artisan income and repeat orders.
